1. Demonstrating Engineering Design in your Roadmap Codument
Posted: Oct 19, 2026, 10:31 AM
----------------------------------------------------------------------
Teams,

As you work on your design roadmap document, please note that for your project to qualify for this course, it is essential to clearly demonstrate the application of engineering design principles. This means that at every decision point where multiple approaches are possible, you must explore and discuss a few viable alternatives, and justify why you selected the chosen path.

Even if your project sponsor has imposed certain specifications (such as technology stacks or specific tools), there will still be areas where you have the flexibility to make decisions. It is your responsibility to identify these areas, evaluate your options, and justify your choices based on credible sources, such as industry best practices, industry standards and guidelines, scholarly articles, or relevant books.

Additional Guidelines for Capstone Project Design

- Document Trade-offs and Justifications: Clearly outline the pros and cons of each design option and explain why the chosen path was selected based on project priorities.
- Perform Risk Analysis: Identify potential risks for each alternative and explain your strategy for mitigating them.
- Embrace Iterative Design: Recognize that design is a dynamic process. Be open to revisiting decisions as new information emerges.
- Prototyping and Testing: Recognize that prototyping may invalidate assumptions you made in early design process.
- Align with Scope and Goals: Ensure that your decisions support the project's objectives, deliverables, budget, and timeline.
- Maintain Communication with Sponsor: Keep the sponsor informed, and address any conflicting requirements transparently.
- Reference Technical Standards: Utilize relevant industry standards, guidelines, and best practices to support your decisions.
- Use Visual Aids: Enhance your presentations with diagrams, charts, or simulations to clearly communicate complex ideas.
- Consider Sustainability and Ethics: Consider reflecting on the environmental, social, and ethical implications of your design choices.

2. Categorizing Deliverables: Must-Haves, Should-Haves, and Nice-to-Haves
Posted: Oct 14, 2026, 9:19 AM
----------------------------------------------------------------------
Hello all,

As you prepare to work on your design roadmap document, it is essential that each project team clearly defines and categorizes their deliverables into three groups: Must-Haves, Should-Haves, and Nice-to-Haves. This categorization serves as a way to set clear expectations for the project's success and progression.

- Must-Haves: These are the non-negotiable, minimum requirements that your team needs to deliver in order to successfully complete the course. Without these, the project is considered incomplete or unsuccessful. Think of these as your core deliverables that demonstrate you've met the essential objectives of your project. Teams should prioritize Must-Haves from the beginning and allocate the necessary resources and time to ensure their completion. The quality of these must be solid, and no compromises should be made on their delivery.

- Should-Haves: These are important features or deliverables that significantly enhance your project but are not absolutely critical for its completion. While the sponsor and your evaluators would be pleased to see the Should-Haves included, not delivering all of them will not impact your overall success in the course. However, delivering on Should-Haves can demonstrate strong planning, efficient resource management, and advanced problem-solving skills, showing that your team has exceeded basic requirements.

- Nice-to-Haves: These are additional, value-adding deliverables that go above and beyond the project's essential scope. Nice-to-Haves are often features or enhancements that superstar teams achieve, reflecting creativity, innovation, and a strong understanding of the problem space. Successfully incorporating Nice-to-Haves is a sign of excellence and can greatly impress sponsors and stakeholders, though they should only be pursued if Must-Haves and Should-Haves have been sufficiently addressed.

Agreement with the Sponsor
It is crucial that these categories and their contents are mutually agreed upon between your team and your sponsor. Engaging in this conversation early on will help align expectations, prioritize efforts, and manage scope effectively. Ensure you have clear documentation that outlines each category, which will guide both your team and the sponsor throughout the project timeline.

Important Note: Remember that while Must-Haves define the minimum success criteria, the Should-Haves and Nice-to-Haves give you opportunities to differentiate your project. Teams should be strategic and realistic about what they can achieve within the project timeline, but also think ambitiously about how they can exceed expectations.

Good luck teams!

3. Services from Makerspace
Posted: Oct 12, 2026, 11:22 PM
----------------------------------------------------------------------
Hello Capstone Students,

As you head into your final project, you've got a whole network of people and spaces here to support you. This is an exciting stretch of your degree, and the Makerspace team is looking forward to helping you bring your ideas to life.

Here's what's available to you:

• Makerspace HQ (https://schulich.libguides.com/m2z/facilities/hq)
General hand tools, build space, 3D printing (https://schulich.libguides.com/m2z/manufacturing/3d-printing), and laser cutting (https://schulich.libguides.com/c.php?g=722137).
  - For 3D printing, refer to the provided slides for how to submit to the Academic Courses queue. Submissions there take priority over the Member queue.

• Manufacturing in the Makerspace Shops (https://schulich.libguides.com/c.php?g=745335)
Manual machining (https://schulich.libguides.com/c.php?g=725732), waterjet (https://schulich.libguides.com/c.php?g=725861), CNC, welding.
  - This is a facility, not a service. Please book a consult (https://schulich.libcal.com/appointments/makerspace-consult) if you plan to fabricate parts, so we can help guide you in how to make your own parts.
  - If you're new to the shop, book an Initial Group Shop Consult.
  - If you're familiar with the shop and just need technical guidance, book a Machine Shop Consult.
  - If you have no machining experience, try to get in early to do training and orientation so you can plan your project timeline properly.

• Electronics Makerspace (https://schulich.libguides.com/c.php?g=737821)
Tools and equipment for electronics prototyping.
  - A series of hands-on workshops (https://schulich.libguides.com/IntroToElectronicPrototyping) such as soldering and PCB fabrication are available throughout the year

• Digital Design Lab (https://schulich.libguides.com/m2z/facilities/ddlab) and VR Lab (https://schulich.libguides.com/c.php?g=725185)
Extended collaborative build spaces, robotics equipment, sewing machines, and 3D visualization tools.

• Consultations (https://schulich.libcal.com/appointments/makerspace-consult)
You can book time with a Makerspace technician to talk through designs, approaches, and next steps.
Undergraduate Lab Technicians (https://schulich.libguides.com/tech-services/support) outside the Makerspace are also available for informal consultation. They won't act as project mentors, but they can help troubleshoot ideas and point you in the right direction.

• Materials and Fasteners
We can provide a reasonable amount of common fasteners and readily available materials at no charge. Chat with us early so we can help you sort out what makes sense for your build. Smaller items such as fasteners are distributed at regular Engineering Store Hours (https://schulich.libguides.com/tech-services/contact-us). For large material stock (e.g., sheet metal or bar), it is best to visit the shop area when ENC 18 (Waterjet) is open (https://schulich.libcal.com/hours/) to determine stock availability before ordering through Engineering Stores.

• Storage
Half-size lockers are available across from the 3D printer wall and the Bill Rosehart Wellness Room (ENA 06). Use the locker request form (https://schulich.libwizard.com/f/capstone-locker-request) if you need one.
For large or long-term storage needs, please coordinate with your professor or project advisor, as we aren't able to store oversized builds in Makerspace areas.

• Contact Information
If you're unsure where to start or who to talk to, send us a note at sse.m2z@ucalgary.ca and we'll guide you from there.

Have fun with this stage of your project. It's one of the most creative and rewarding parts of your degree, and we're excited to see what you build.

Happy making

The Schulich Makerspace Team
