5. Typhoon HIL Remote Farm Now Available
Posted: Oct 20, 2026, 9:23 PM
----------------------------------------------------------------------
Hi everyone,

The Typhoon remote farm is now running. You can use it to access the HIL lab PCs from off campus.

How to connect

- Go to https://labstats.ucalgary.ca/schulich.html
- Read the instructions at the top of the page carefully and follow them.
- Scroll down to the list of computers and click an available one to get its connection steps. In short, you log in at securelabs.ucalgary.ca, click RDP, enter that computer's address, and open the .rdp file that downloads.
- Log in with your UCalgary username and password.

Software on the remote computers

- Typhoon HIL Control Center is already installed, since these are the same lab PCs you used in the Orientation Lab.
- PLECS is not preinstalled, but you can install it yourself without admin rights. PLECS licenses are tied to a specific computer, so the license on your personal PC won't work on a lab PC. Request a new license from Plexim using the student license information on D2L (the same information you used for your personal PC). Your install and license will only work on that one machine, so note which computer it is and use it again when you can.

LaunchPads

The LaunchPads are not connected to the HIL units yet. We'll add them by Thursday. I'll post a follow-up once the LaunchPads are connected.

Please note

- There are 8 computers in the farm. A computer disappears from the list while someone is connected to it. If none are listed, all of them are in use, so please try again later.
- When you're finished, sign out. Closing the Remote Desktop window does not free up the computer. To logout, enter Ctrl+Alt+End inside the remote session and then log off. A computer only goes back on the list after you sign out, so if you forget, it stays unavailable to your classmates.
- Since there are only 8 machines, please don't stay connected while you aren't actively working. Expect them to be busier in the days before the project bench sessions, so plan your remote time around that.

If you have trouble connecting, post in the discussion forum or contact Mohammad Islam.

12. Quiz 2 (Tuesday, October 13): scope, aids and how to prepare
Posted: Oct 3, 2026, 12:29 PM
----------------------------------------------------------------------
Hi everyone,

Quiz 2 is on Tuesday, October 13 at 10 AM in SB 146. It runs 50 minutes and is worth 10 % of your grade.

Scope

- Lecture 6, AC fundamentals: phasors, solving a circuit one frequency at a time, real and reactive power, rms of a waveform with harmonics, and THD.
- Lecture 7, single-phase inverters: the H-bridge and its switch states, square-wave operation, phase-shift control, bipolar and unipolar sinusoidal PWM, and where their harmonics sit.
- Topic 8, power balance and the dc link: power balance between the ac and dc sides of an inverter, and what the dc-link capacitor does.
- Topic 9, power semiconductor devices: which current directions and voltage polarities each device handles.
- Lab 2: the single-phase H-bridge inverter experiments.

I've decided not to include rectifiers on this quiz. We only started them at the end of the October 1 lecture; they will be covered in a later quiz.

Permitted aids

- A non-programmable calculator.
- One double-sided, letter-size cheat sheet. It can be handwritten or printed, and you may copy anything onto it, including material from the lecture slides.

As a reminder, smart glasses are not permitted. If your only glasses are smart glasses, you may wear them, but we will provide tape, and you must use it to cover all camera lenses before the quiz starts.

No formulas are provided on the quiz. Anything you want to have in front of you must be on your cheat sheet. You do not need to copy the PWM harmonic table onto your sheet; it will be provided if needed.

What to expect

As in Quiz 1, the questions test reasoning, not formula lookup: expect to apply the ideas to circuits and operating points you have not seen before.

Show your working for every part. A correct final number with no working shown earns at most half the marks for that part.

How to prepare

- Work the Topics 6 to 9 problems in the new problems.html file posted on D2L. Download it and open it in your browser, then click the Quiz 2 filter button to show only the problems for this quiz. 15. Quiz 1 (Tuesday, September 29): scope, aids and how to prepare
Posted: Sep 25, 2026, 11:59 AM
----------------------------------------------------------------------
Hi everyone,

Quiz 1 is on Tuesday, September 29 at 10 AM in SB 146. It runs 50 minutes and is worth 10% of your grade.

Scope:

- DC-DC converters from Lectures 2 to 5: the buck, boost, and buck-boost converters, and the flyback converter.
- Inductor volt-second balance, capacitor charge balance, and the small-ripple approximation.
- Inductor current and voltage waveforms, current ripple, and output voltage ripple.
- Continuous and discontinuous conduction (CCM and DCM), the boundary between them, and synchronous versus diode rectification.
- Switch voltage and current stresses.
- The flyback converter modelled as a magnetizing inductance plus an ideal transformer.
- Lab 1: the open-loop buck and closed-loop boost experiments.

Magnetics (core sizing, flux, leakage inductance, open- and short-circuit tests) and AC fundamentals are not on this quiz. They will be covered in a later quiz.

Permitted aids

- A non-programmable calculator.
- One double-sided, letter-size cheat sheet. It can be handwritten or printed, and you may copy anything onto it, including material from the lecture slides.

No formulas are provided on the quiz. Anything you want to have in front of you must be on your cheat sheet.

Smart glasses are not permitted. If your only glasses are smart glasses, you may wear them, but we will provide tape, and you must use it to cover all camera lenses before the quiz starts.

What to expect

The quiz is not a formula-lookup exercise, so a well-filled cheat sheet on its own will not carry you. The questions ask you to:

- interpret measured waveforms like the ones you captured in Lab 1;
- apply volt-second and charge balance to circuits, including ones you have not seen before;
- decide which operating mode a converter is in, and which assumption breaks when conditions change;
- justify design choices briefly.

Show your working for every part. That means writing down how you got each answer: which interval or circuit you analysed, the equation you set up, or what you read from a waveform. A correct final number with no working shown earns at most half the marks for that part.

How to prepare

- Work through the assigned problems for Lectures 2 to 5 on D2L, starting from the balance principles rather than a formula.
- Revisit your Lab 1 results. For each waveform you captured, make sure you can explain what the converter was doing in each interval and why.
- Building your cheat sheet is part of your preparation. A summary of how to set up the analysis (which intervals, what the inductor sees in each, how to apply the balance conditions) will help you more than a list of final formulas.

Good luck, and see you on Tuesday.

Phil
