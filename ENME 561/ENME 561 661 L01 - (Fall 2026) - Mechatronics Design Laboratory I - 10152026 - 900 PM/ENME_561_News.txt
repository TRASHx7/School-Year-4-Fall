2. Important Reminder: AI Use and Academic Integrity
Posted: Oct 23, 2026, 3:15 PM
----------------------------------------------------------------------
Please ensure that all graphs included in your reports are generated using code that you have written and understand, based on the data you collected during your experiments or simulations.

AI tools can be helpful for learning and coding support, but you must understand, verify, and take responsibility for the results you submit. AI generated plots and analysis, or using AI to perform data analysis without understanding how the results were obtained may constitute a violation of academic integrity. Any such cases will be addressed in accordance with university policies.

Please also remember that although only one group member may submit the report, all group members are jointly responsible for the content, accuracy, and academic integrity of the submitted work.

As stated in the course outline, under "Transparent Use": AI tools may be used to support your learning and content creation, with appropriate attribution. You may use AI for drafting, problem-solving support, or code assistance, provided that you critically evaluate and revise all outputs. You are responsible for the accuracy and integrity of your submission. All use of AI, including prompts and tool identification, must be clearly documented through an AI use statement included with your work.

The goal is to help you develop your own skills and understanding. If you have questions or need support, please don't hesitate to reach out!

10. Take Advantage of the Makerspace Trainings While You Can
Posted: Sep 4, 2026, 2:23 PM
----------------------------------------------------------------------
Hi everyone,

This has nothing to do with your grade or any course deliverable. It is simply some advice from someone working in this field.

The Schulich makerspaces offer hands-on training opportunities that are available to you as a student. Once you graduate, access to many of these resources and trainings may no longer be available.

Mechatronics is a hands-on discipline, and practical skills can make a real difference. We cover many hands-on concepts and activities as much as possible in ENME 561 and ENME 562. However, there are many additional practical skills and specialized trainings offered through the Schulich Makerspaces that we are not able to cover within the scope of these courses. These opportunities can help you expand your hands-on experience beyond the classroom.

Some trainings that may be particularly relevant to you include:

- Electronics & PCB (Electronics Makerspace, ENG 29): Soldering THT, Soldering SMT I and II (Oven), PCB Milling
- Rapid Prototyping: 3D Printing and Laser Cutting
- Machining & Structures (Machine Shop): Access Training, Lathe and Milling Machine, CNC
- Robotics related training

My practical advice:

- Start with the Machine Shop Access training. It is the prerequisite for using many of the machines in the shop.
- Don't wait until you have an immediate project that requires the skill.
- Consider taking at least one training outside your immediate needs.

You can see the available workshops and register here:

https://schulich.libcal.com/calendar/workshops

If you have time this term, I strongly encourage you to take advantage of at least one of these opportunities!

Thanks,

Bisheban
